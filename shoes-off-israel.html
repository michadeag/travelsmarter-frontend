Do You Take Your Shoes Off When Entering a Home in Israel?

Removing shoes when entering a home is not a standard or widely expected custom in Israel — most Israeli households keep shoes on indoors. You may occasionally encounter homes where it's preferred, but there's no strong national norm around it.

Israel doesn't have the kind of ingrained shoe-removal culture found in Japan, much of East Asia, or some Middle Eastern countries. That said, individual families differ: some households — particularly those who are more traditionally observant or of certain ethnic backgrounds — may prefer shoes off, but this varies widely rather than following a consistent rule. In terms of other indoor spaces, shoe removal is not typically expected at restaurants, offices, or shops. Religious sites are a separate matter: mosques require shoes off, while synagogues and churches generally do not.

💡 When you arrive at someone's home, glance at the entrance for a pile of shoes — if you see several pairs lined up near the door, take yours off; if not, keep them on and you'll almost certainly be fine.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to remove my shoes at a synagogue in Israel?

Generally no — shoes are worn inside synagogues in Israel. The exception is Yom Kippur, when some traditional Jews remove leather shoes as part of the day's observances, but this applies to the worshippers themselves rather than being a rule for visitors.

What about entering a mosque in Israel?

Yes, you will be expected to remove your shoes before entering a mosque in Israel, as is standard in mosques worldwide. This applies to visitors of all backgrounds, so wear easy-to-remove footwear if you're planning to visit one.

How do I politely ask whether I should take my shoes off at an Israeli home?

A simple 'Should I take my shoes off?' is perfectly acceptable and will not cause any awkwardness. Most Israeli hosts will tell you directly either way — Israelis tend to be frank and won't be offended by the question.
