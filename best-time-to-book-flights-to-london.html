Flying to London from the US

London–Heathrow (LHR) is the best-connected European gateway from the US, with nonstop service from more than 20 cities including JFK, EWR, ORD, ATL, IAD, BOS, MIA, SFO, LAX, SEA, and PHL. British Airways, Virgin Atlantic, American, Delta, and United all fly the route, with flight times of roughly 7 to 8 hours eastbound. Norse Atlantic also flies budget nonstop service into London–Gatwick (LGW) from a smaller set of cities.

Peak season (June–August) brings the highest fares, plus a fare bump around Wimbledon in early July. Shoulder season — April–May and September–October — offers milder weather and noticeably better pricing. Winter (November–March, outside the Christmas/New Year spike) is the cheapest stretch of the year.

Budget vs. premium carriers on this route

Because London is one of the most heavily contested transatlantic routes, fares are unusually sensitive to booking timing. Budget carriers sell their lowest fares first and raise prices quickly; full-service carriers hold back more inventory and often open better fares — including premium economy — in the middle of the booking window.

Frequently asked questions

When should I book flights to London for the cheapest fare?

4–6 weeks out on full-service carriers, or 3–5 weeks out on budget carriers. This is a high-competition route, so fares move fast once your window opens.

What is the cheapest time of year to fly to London?

January–March and November are the cheapest months. April–May and September–October give you good weather at a lower price than summer.

Is it cheaper to fly to London on a budget or full-service airline?

Norse Atlantic often has the lowest headline fare, but full-service carriers frequently beat it if you book in the middle of your window rather than last minute.

Do I need a visa to visit London from the US?

No visa is required for short visits, but US travelers need a UK Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA), obtained online before departure.
