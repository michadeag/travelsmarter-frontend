Do You Take Your Shoes Off When Entering a Home in Hong Kong?

Removing shoes when entering a home in Hong Kong is common but not universal — many households expect it, so follow the host's lead or check for a shoe rack at the entrance as your cue.

A visible pile of shoes or a shoe rack near the front door is the clearest signal that you should take yours off too. The practice is influenced by both hygiene preferences and Chinese cultural norms, and it is more consistently observed in traditional or older households than in some Westernized or expat homes. Unlike Japan, there is no deep ritual significance attached to it — it is primarily a cleanliness habit. Outside private homes, the norm does not typically extend to restaurants, shops, or most other public indoor spaces in Hong Kong.

💡 Glance at the entrance when you arrive — if you see a row of shoes and slippers by the door, remove yours without waiting to be asked.

Frequently asked questions

Will my host be offended if I don't take my shoes off?

In households where shoe removal is expected, keeping them on can be seen as inconsiderate, though hosts may not say anything directly. Following the visual cues at the entrance — a shoe rack, slippers left out — is the safest way to avoid any awkwardness without needing to ask.

Do you need to remove shoes in traditional Chinese temples or shrines in Hong Kong?

Most Chinese temples in Hong Kong do not require visitors to remove shoes, unlike some religious sites in other parts of Asia. You can generally enter in your normal footwear, but check for any posted signs or follow what other visitors are doing if you are unsure.

Are guest slippers usually provided in Hong Kong homes?

Many households that expect shoe removal will have a set of guest slippers near the entrance, though this is not guaranteed. It is worth wearing clean socks if you are visiting someone's home, just in case slippers are not available or do not fit well.
