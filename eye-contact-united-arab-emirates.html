Is Direct Eye Contact Polite or Rude in United Arab Emirates?

Moderate eye contact is generally expected and respectful in the UAE, but prolonged or intense eye contact — especially with elders, senior authority figures, or across genders — can feel inappropriate or aggressive.

In Emirati culture, some eye contact signals attentiveness and sincerity, but staring or holding a gaze too long is considered rude or confrontational, particularly when speaking to someone older or of higher status. Gender dynamics also matter: sustained direct eye contact between unrelated men and women can be seen as forward or disrespectful. The UAE's multicultural workforce means you'll encounter a wide range of norms depending on who you're speaking with, but erring toward moderate, natural eye contact is broadly safe across settings.

💡 Make natural, intermittent eye contact as you would in most professional settings, but glance away periodically — especially if speaking with an elder, a senior official, or someone of the opposite gender you don't know well.

Frequently asked questions

Does the rule about eye contact differ between locals (Emiratis) and expats in the UAE?

Traditional Emirati cultural norms around eye contact tend to be more conservative, particularly regarding gender and seniority. Expats from Western or South Asian backgrounds may have different personal expectations. In practice, matching the other person's level of eye contact is a reliable way to avoid offense in mixed settings.

Is eye contact during business meetings treated differently than in casual conversation?

In formal business meetings, some eye contact is expected and signals engagement, but the same moderation applies — you don't need to maintain an unbroken gaze to appear confident or trustworthy. If you're meeting with a senior Emirati executive, occasional breaks in eye contact show deference rather than weakness.

Should women visiting the UAE be especially careful about eye contact with men?

Yes, as a practical precaution, female visitors may want to keep eye contact brief and neutral when interacting with men they don't know, particularly in traditional or conservative settings. This isn't a strict rule, and many everyday interactions — with service staff, shopkeepers, or colleagues — are relaxed, but erring on the side of modesty is unlikely to offend anyone.
