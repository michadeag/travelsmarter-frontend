Which Hand Gestures Should You Avoid in Ireland?

Ireland follows general Western norms for hand gestures — there are no well-known gesture taboos that differ significantly from what visitors from the US, UK, or Europe would expect.

The thumbs up, OK sign, V-sign (used palm-out as a peace/victory sign), and a wave or nod to beckon someone are all understood in their standard Western senses in Ireland. The two-fingered reverse-V (back of hand facing outward) carries the same rude connotation it does in the UK and Australia, so that's worth knowing if you're American and unfamiliar with it. There is no locally distinctive gesture tradition that would cause a well-meaning visitor to accidentally offend someone.

💡 If you're American, just be aware that holding up two fingers in a V with the back of your hand facing outward is considered rude across Ireland and Britain — stick to the palm-outward version if you mean 'peace' or 'two, please.'

Frequently asked questions

Is the 'OK' sign offensive in Ireland?

No, the OK sign (fingertip circle with three fingers raised) is understood in its standard positive sense in Ireland and is not considered offensive. There is no Irish-specific taboo attached to it.

Is the reverse two-fingered V-sign rude in Ireland?

Yes — a V-sign made with the back of the hand facing outward is considered an offensive, vulgar gesture in Ireland, just as it is in the UK. This is mainly relevant to American visitors who may not be familiar with the distinction, since in the US the orientation rarely matters.

Is beckoning someone with one finger considered rude in Ireland?

Beckoning with a single crooked finger can come across as curt or condescending in many social situations, but this is a general etiquette point rather than a culturally specific Irish taboo. A small wave or a nod is a more polite and universally understood way to get someone's attention.
