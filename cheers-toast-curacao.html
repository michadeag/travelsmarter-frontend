How Do You Say "Cheers" in Curaçao, and Do You Clink Glasses?

In Curaçao, the most common toast is 'Salú!' (from Papiamentu, meaning 'health/cheers'), and clinking glasses is standard and expected in social settings.

Papiamentu is the local creole language of Curaçao, and 'Salú' is the everyday toast you'll hear at bars, restaurants, and family gatherings. Dutch speakers may say 'Proost' and Spanish-influenced settings might use 'Salud,' reflecting the island's multicultural heritage. Clinking glasses is the norm, and like much of the Caribbean and Latin-influenced world, making eye contact while clinking is considered polite — avoiding it is sometimes jokingly said to bring bad luck. This is fairly consistent across the island, though it's a social custom rather than a rigid rule.

💡 Lead with 'Salú!' when toasting — locals will appreciate the effort with the Papiamentu word, and it's easy to remember since it sounds close to the Spanish 'Salud.'

Frequently asked questions

Is it rude not to clink glasses with everyone at the table?

It's considered good manners to clink with each person at the table rather than just raising your glass generally, especially in smaller, more intimate groups. Missing someone out isn't a serious offense, but making the effort is appreciated. Eye contact while clinking adds a friendly, respectful touch.

Do people in Curaçao speak Papiamentu, Dutch, or Spanish day-to-day?

Papiamentu is the mother tongue for most native Curaçaoans and the language of everyday life, while Dutch is the official language used in government and education. English and Spanish are also widely spoken, especially in tourism contexts. You'll likely hear all four on a single evening out.

What drinks are typically toasted with in Curaçao?

The island's most famous drink is Blue Curaçao liqueur, often served in cocktails, but local beer (Polar and Amstel Bright are popular) and rum-based drinks are the everyday staples for toasting. Wine is common at restaurants too. There's no single 'official' toast drink — 'Salú' works with whatever is in your glass.
