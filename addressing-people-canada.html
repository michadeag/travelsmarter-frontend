First Names or Titles? How to Address People in Canada

First names are used almost immediately in most Canadian contexts — formal titles and surnames are rarely expected beyond specific professional settings like medicine or law.

Canada's workplace and social culture leans strongly informal, and switching to first names within minutes of meeting someone is completely normal and not considered rude or presumptuous. This holds across most of the country, though Quebec can be slightly more formal in professional settings, particularly in older or more traditional workplaces. Academic and medical contexts are a partial exception — professors and doctors are often addressed by title until they signal otherwise.

💡 If you're unsure, listen for how a Canadian introduces themselves: if they give only their first name, that's your cue to use it right away.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use 'Mr.' or 'Ms.' when meeting a Canadian colleague for the first time?

In most Canadian workplaces, using Mr. or Ms. would actually feel unusually stiff and might even create mild awkwardness. Defaulting to first names from the start is the safer, more natural choice in business settings. The main exceptions are formal legal, medical, or government contexts where titles carry professional weight.

Is Quebec different from the rest of Canada when it comes to formality?

Quebec can be marginally more formal in some professional environments, and older or more traditional workplaces may retain a preference for titles a little longer. That said, the overall trend even in Quebec is toward informality, and most younger colleagues or casual professional settings follow the same first-name-quickly pattern as the rest of Canada. When in doubt, take your cue from how the other person introduces themselves.

Does this apply to written communication like emails too, or just in person?

Yes, the informality extends to email as well — it's common to see Canadians open a first email to a new contact with just the person's first name, or even no salutation at all in fast-moving exchanges. If someone signs their email with only their first name, that's a clear signal they're comfortable with informal address. Starting with 'Dear Mr./Ms. [Surname]' won't offend anyone, but it may feel more formal than the situation calls for.
