Which Side Do People Walk On in Chile?

There's no strong, consistent convention for which side of the sidewalk people keep to in Chile — pedestrian flow is largely unstructured, especially outside of transit hubs.

Chile drives on the right side of the road, but this doesn't translate into a reliable right-side walking norm on footpaths. In busy Santiago metro stations and some shopping mall escalators you may see signage or informal pressure to keep right, but on general sidewalks people move fluidly without a dominant pattern. This is broadly similar to pedestrian behavior in most other Latin American countries rather than something uniquely Chilean.

💡 In busy metro stations or on escalators, glance for posted signs or follow what the crowd ahead of you is doing — those spots are the exception where a local norm does occasionally appear.

Frequently asked questions

Do Santiago's metro escalators have a keep-right or keep-left rule?

Some escalators in busier Santiago metro stations have signage asking people to stand to the right and leave the left lane free for those walking up, similar to conventions in other large cities. However, compliance varies and it isn't universally enforced, so follow the crowd at each specific escalator rather than assuming a blanket rule applies everywhere.

Is pedestrian traffic in Chilean shopping malls or markets more organized?

Not particularly — malls and markets in Chile tend to have the same relaxed, fluid foot traffic you'd find in most of the region. There's no strong cultural expectation of keeping to one side in those settings, and visitors shouldn't worry about inadvertently breaking a local norm.

Will locals be bothered if I walk on the 'wrong' side of the sidewalk?

No — because there isn't a firmly established correct side, nobody is likely to notice or care. The practical concern is simply staying aware of oncoming pedestrians in busy areas, as you would anywhere, rather than trying to follow a specific directional convention.
