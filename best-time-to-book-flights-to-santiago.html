Flying to Santiago from the US

Santiago has nonstop service from ATL, MIA, and JFK on LATAM, American, and Delta, with flight times around 9 to 10 hours.

Santiago is in the Southern Hemisphere: Chilean summer (December–February) is peak for city and wine-country tourism, warm and dry. Chilean winter (June–August) is ski season in the nearby Andes — a different kind of peak demand for winter-sports travelers — while being the off-season for general city tourism. Shoulder season (March–May, September–November) offers good value.

Budget vs. premium carriers on this route

There's no dedicated low-cost carrier on this nonstop route — LATAM, American, and Delta compete on price, so booking within the optimal window matters more than airline choice, and depends on whether you're chasing summer city travel or winter ski season.

Frequently asked questions

When should I book flights to Santiago for the cheapest fare?

4 to 6 weeks out on full-service carriers, or 3 to 5 weeks out on lower fare classes.

What is the cheapest time of year to fly to Santiago?

Shoulder season (March–May, September–November) tends to offer the best value. Chilean summer (December–February) is peak for city tourism and the Andes ski season (June–August) creates its own winter demand spike.

Is it cheaper to fly to Santiago on a budget or full-service airline?

No dedicated budget carrier flies this nonstop route — LATAM, American, and Delta compete on price, so timing matters more than airline choice.

Do I need a visa to visit Santiago from the US?

US citizens can currently visit Chile visa-free for short tourist stays, typically up to 90 days.
