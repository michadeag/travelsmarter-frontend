Flying to Milan from the US

Milan–Malpensa (MXP) has solid nonstop service from the US, with direct flights from JFK, EWR, ORD, ATL, and MIA on ITA Airways (Italy's flag carrier), Delta, American, and United. Flight times run around 9 hours eastbound. Milan doesn't have a dedicated low-cost transatlantic carrier, so all nonstop options are full-service airlines.

Milan sees two distinct fare spikes tied to its role as a global fashion and design capital: Milan Fashion Week (late February–early March and late September) and Milan Design Week (April) both drive up hotel and flight prices well beyond typical seasonal patterns. Summer (June–August) is otherwise the peak tourist season and the most expensive stretch for leisure travel, while November and January–February (outside Fashion Week) tend to be the quietest and cheapest.

Budget vs. premium carriers on this route

With no dedicated budget carrier flying nonstop to Milan, "budget" fares are simply the lowest economy fare class ITA Airways, Delta, American, or United release — and these get scooped up fastest around Fashion Week and Design Week. Booking 3–5 weeks out gives you the best shot at the cheapest seats outside those events; 4–6 weeks out tends to be optimal for premium economy and business-class fares before last-minute price increases.

Frequently asked questions

When should I book flights to Milan for the cheapest fare?

4–6 weeks out on full-service carriers, or 3–5 weeks out for the lowest discounted economy fares. Book earlier if your dates overlap with Fashion Week (late February–early March, late September) or Design Week (April).

What is the cheapest time of year to fly to Milan?

November and January–February (outside Fashion Week) are typically cheapest. Summer is peak tourist season, and fares spike sharply during fashion events regardless of season.

Is it cheaper to fly to Milan on a budget or full-service airline?

There's no dedicated low-cost carrier on this route — the comparison is between ITA Airways, Delta, American, and United fare classes. Booking 4–6 weeks out generally beats last-minute pricing.

Do I need a visa to visit Milan from the US?

US citizens can currently visit visa-free for up to 90 days in the Schengen area. The EU's ETIAS travel authorization has been rolling out — check the current requirement close to your travel date.
