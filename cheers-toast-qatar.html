How Do You Say "Cheers" in Qatar, and Do You Clink Glasses?

Qatar is a majority-Muslim country where alcohol is heavily restricted, so there is no widely-used local toasting phrase or established glass-clinking culture in everyday social life.

Alcohol is only legally available to non-Muslim residents and tourists in licensed hotel bars, certain private clubs, and the government-run Qatar Distribution Company. In those settings, expatriates and tourists may use English toasts like 'Cheers' or toasts from their own cultures — there is no distinct Qatari Arabic toasting phrase in common use because public drinking simply isn't part of local tradition. If you do drink in a licensed venue, clinking glasses and eye contact follow no uniquely Qatari rules; general international bar etiquette applies.

💡 In licensed hotel bars, a simple 'Cheers' is perfectly understood and appropriate — don't feel pressure to use an Arabic phrase, as none is conventionally established for this purpose.

Frequently asked questions

Can I drink alcohol in Qatar at all as a tourist?

Yes, tourists can drink alcohol in Qatar, but only in licensed venues such as hotel bars and restaurants that hold a liquor licence. You cannot drink in public spaces, and purchasing alcohol for private consumption requires a permit. The rules tightened and then partially relaxed around the 2022 FIFA World Cup, so always check current regulations before your visit.

Is it offensive to drink in front of Qatari locals?

Drinking openly in public or in non-licensed settings would be considered disrespectful and is also illegal. Within the clearly designated licensed venues, locals generally understand that tourists and expatriates drink, but it is still courteous to be discreet and avoid rowdy behaviour. Ramadan brings additional restrictions, so drinking even in licensed venues may be limited during daylight hours in that period.

Are there any Arabic phrases useful for social occasions in Qatar if alcohol isn't involved?

For non-alcoholic toasts or simply raising a glass of juice or water in a celebratory moment, some Arabic speakers say 'Sahtain' (صحتين), meaning 'double health' or 'to your health,' similar to 'cheers' in spirit. It is used across the Arab world informally and would be understood in Qatar, though it is not a deeply ingrained local toasting ritual. Using it in a warm, friendly way would generally be appreciated rather than seen as awkward.
