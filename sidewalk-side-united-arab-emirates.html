Which Side Do People Walk On in United Arab Emirates?

There is no strong or consistent pedestrian convention in the UAE — sidewalk and staircase flow is largely unstructured, with no dominant keep-left or keep-right norm.

The UAE drives on the right side of the road, but this hasn't translated into a clear matching pedestrian convention the way it has in some other countries. The population is extremely diverse — roughly 88–89% are expatriates from dozens of countries with differing home-country norms — so no single walking-side habit has taken hold. In malls, airports, and metro stations you may see informal signage or queue markings that guide flow in specific spots, but out on general sidewalks it's genuinely ad hoc.

💡 In busy indoor spaces like Dubai Mall or Abu Dhabi's malls, look for floor arrows or lane markings near escalators and entrances — those specific spots do have guided flow even if the broader streets don't.

Frequently asked questions

Do escalators in UAE malls have a stand-left, walk-right (or similar) rule?

There's no universally observed escalator etiquette enforced across the UAE. In practice, standing behavior on escalators is mixed and inconsistent, especially given the multinational crowd. You'll sometimes see people standing on both sides without issue, though busier metro stations in Dubai occasionally see informal walk-left, stand-right behavior emerge during rush hour.

Is pedestrian flow more organized inside Dubai Metro stations?

Dubai Metro stations have some of the clearest pedestrian guidance in the country, with painted floor arrows and designated entry/exit lanes at turnstiles. In those marked areas, people generally follow the indicated direction reasonably well. Outside of explicitly marked zones, flow becomes unstructured again.

Will walking on the 'wrong' side cause friction with locals or other pedestrians?

No — because there's no established norm to violate, nobody will react negatively. Just stay alert and be willing to step aside when paths converge, the same common-sense courtesy you'd apply anywhere with a mixed and dense crowd.
