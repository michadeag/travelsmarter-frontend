Which Hand Gestures Should You Avoid in Chile?

Chile follows general Western norms for most common hand gestures — there are no major gesture taboos here that would catch a typical tourist off guard.

The thumbs-up, OK sign, and V/peace sign are all understood in their standard positive or neutral senses in Chile without the offensive connotations those gestures carry in some other countries. Beckoning with a single curled finger is considered somewhat rude or condescending, as it is in much of Latin America and indeed many Western cultures — it's better to catch someone's attention with a small wave. The middle finger is universally understood as offensive, consistent with most of the Western world.

💡 If you need to beckon a waiter or someone across the room, use a small, open-handed wave rather than a single pointing or curling finger — it reads as more polite and is the local norm.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 'OK' sign offensive in Chile like it is in some other countries?

No — in Chile the OK sign is generally understood as a positive or neutral gesture meaning 'okay' or 'good,' without the vulgar connotation it carries in places like Brazil or Turkey. You can use it without concern in everyday situations.

Does the V-sign / peace sign mean something different in Chile?

No notable difference here. The V-sign with palm facing outward reads as 'peace' or 'victory,' just as it does across most of the Western world. There is no particular Chilean sensitivity around this gesture.

Are there any Chilean-specific gestures I should learn to understand locals?

A few local gestures exist — for example, tapping the underside of the elbow with one hand can imply someone is stingy. These are relatively minor and you're unlikely to encounter them as a tourist, but noticing them can add some cultural color to your trip.
