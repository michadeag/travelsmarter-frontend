Frequently asked questions

How much does an ATM withdrawal cost in United States?

Typically $3-6 in flat ATM fees per withdrawal in United States, regardless of how much you take out. Bank-branded ATMs sometimes charge both a network fee and your home bank's out-of-network fee — using your own bank's ATM network avoids double fees.

What is Dynamic Currency Conversion, and is it common in United States?

Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC) is when an ATM or card terminal offers to charge you in your home currency instead of the local one — it applies a worse exchange rate with a hidden markup. DCC prompts are rare in United States. Always choose to pay in the local currency.

Should I withdraw a lot of cash at once in United States?

Generally yes — since ATM fees in United States are typically a flat amount per withdrawal, taking out a larger sum less often costs you less in total fees than several small withdrawals.

Will my home bank charge extra fees on top of the local ATM fee in United States?

Possibly — many banks charge their own foreign transaction and/or out-of-network ATM fee in addition to whatever United States's local ATM charges, so check your bank's fee schedule before you travel.
